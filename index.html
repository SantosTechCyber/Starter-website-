Protect your money and your PIN

Scammers copy M-PESA and bank messages to trick people. These checks take less than a minute.

Check the sender

Real M-PESA and bank messages come from the official sender name in your messages app, not a random number or a link.

  • Compare the message with your own statement in the official app
  • Be wary of messages that create urgency

Never share your PIN

M-PESA staff and banks will never ask for your PIN, M-PESA PIN, OTP, or verification code, by call, SMS, or WhatsApp.

  • Hang up and call the official number on the back of your card or the company website
  • Do not type your PIN into any link

Avoid links in money messages

Shortened links such as saf.cx or bit.ly can hide fake login pages that steal your details.

  • Open the official app instead of tapping the link
  • Type the website address yourself if you must visit one

Tell a fake message apart

Common warning signs include spelling errors, unknown numbers, requests for money to "release" a payment, and prizes you never entered.

  • If unsure, delete the message and check your balance in the app
  • Report scam numbers to your provider

Think before you invest or sign up

Offers promising quick money, guaranteed winnings, or easy referral income often lead to losses.

  • Research any app or platform independently before using it
  • If it sounds too good to be true, it probably is

What to do if you were tricked

Act fast. The sooner you report, the better the chance of stopping further losses.

  • Change your PIN and passwords right away
  • Call your bank or M-PESA support on their official number
  • Send me the details and I will help you check your phone
